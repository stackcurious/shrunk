## Verdict

Yes — this now reads as an Apple app, and the change is not cosmetic. The restyle went past skinning:
Settings and Notification preferences are real `Form`/`List` sections with proper footers, every empty
and gated state is a `ContentUnavailableView`, the tab bar and sheets are stock, the meter and cards
sit on `systemGroupedBackground`, dark mode falls out of the semantic palette rather than being
bolted on, and the tracked caps labels, mono display face and hand-drawn capsules are genuinely gone
— I grepped for all of them and found nothing. Wave 1 delivered the harder half of the promise: every
loaded Result now states a fact and offers an action, the single-snapshot screen is a first-class
screen with a real cost-per-ounce and a watchable baseline, and all four `WatchOutcome` branches give
the user something they can see. The Watch bug that started this is fixed and pinned by tests.
So *does every scan pay off* — yes, in the code. But not yet on the device: the deployed Worker is
still serving the pre-audit catalogue, so the Browse tab's number-one "worst offender" today is a
−94 % figure that is arithmetic garbage, sitting two rows above a Toblerone case the project itself
retracted yesterday — and one of the six App Store shots publishes that same retracted claim. Those
two, plus a Result screen that truncates its own Then→Now numbers and overlaps its cost bars the
moment a user turns text size up, are what stand between this and a build I'd put in front of
testers. None of them is deep; B1 is a deploy, B2 is a re-shoot, B3 is three fixed frames. Fix those
and the should-fix list (the sheet titles and the two "set your store" dead-ends are the ones a
tester will actually hit), and I'd ship it.
