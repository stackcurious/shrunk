### Method

The full Mouse Print\* downsizing archive was enumerated from
`https://www.mouseprint.org/category/downsiz/` pages 1–44 — **120 articles,
2006–2026** — downloaded and converted to text (309 KB), then searched locally.
That made per-entry verification cost roughly zero extra fetches and covered the
whole corpus rather than whatever a search engine surfaced. Consumer World and
general news search were the fallback.

GTINs were re-checked against **USDA FoodData Central**, **Open Food Facts** and
the **Kroger Products API**. Kroger was called **directly at `api.kroger.com`**
with the client credentials in `~/.config/shrunk/`, deliberately *not* through
`/v1/kroger/product` — that route runs with `KROGER_PERSIST="on"` and writes an
observation plus a `price_snapshots` row per call, which would have polluted D1
and skewed the very hit-rate number this report cites.

### The bar an entry had to clear

1. A page that **actually loads** (HTTP 200, re-checked here) and **states both
   net contents** for **that** product. A percentage, a "lost two ounces", or a
   headline figure that is not in the body text is **not** enough.
2. The GTIN is real, confirmed in at least one product database, and identifies
   the **product line the source names**.
3. The current package is **not larger** than the claimed after-size. Equal is
   confirmation; smaller means it shrank again (the entry still records the
   documented step); **larger is a contradiction and the entry is removed**.

### Date convention (also written into the file's own `notes`)

`history[1].date` is the **publication date of the cited article** — the month
the smaller package was documented on shelf. `history[0].date` is twelve months
earlier, an approximation for "the size the source says it used to be". No date
in this file is presented as more precise than that. This replaces the previous
undocumented, unverifiable dates.

## 5. Hit rate — and why it is 17/25, not 25/25

```
$ python3 scripts/hit_rate.py --api https://shrunk-api.stackcurious.workers.dev --curated data/trending.json
found=25/25 with_history=25/25 shrink_detected=17/25
```

**All eight non-shrinks read exactly `no shrink (+0.0%)`.** Not one is a
contradiction. Each is a third observation from USDA FoodData Central (or Kroger)
that reports *precisely* the curated after-size, which flattens the last pair:

```
Wheat Thins Original      2020-05-10  453.592 g curated (16 oz)
                          2021-05-10  396.893 g curated (14 oz)
                          2023-06-29  396.893 g fdc     (14 oz/396 g)   <- identical
```

Fage is the clearest case: **USDA independently confirms both endpoints**
(35.3 oz in 2021-07, 32 oz in 2022-12) and the entry still scores "no shrink",
because the newest two observations are the USDA 32 oz row and the curated 32 oz
row.

Compare with the pre-verification run, where the non-shrinks were genuine
*disagreements* — Wheat Thins +3.7 %, Cheez-It +10.5 %, Goldfish +38.9 %, Honey
Nut Cheerios +22.1 %, Breyers +50.0 %, Quaker +25.4 %. Every one of those entries
has been removed or corrected. **No independent database now disagrees with any
of the 25 claims**, which is the property the brief actually asked for.

### The remaining gap is a product bug, not a data bug — and it is not mine to fix here

`hit_rate.verdict()` and `ShrinkDetector.analyze` both compare the **last two**
observations without collapsing consecutive equal sizes. So an observation that
*confirms* the shrink reads as "unchanged". A shopper scanning Wheat Thins today
would see **"Unchanged"** rather than "Shrank 12.5 % since 2020", purely because
USDA agrees with us.

`seed_curated.py` already collapses consecutive equal sizes *within* the curated
points (`SAME_SIZE_TOLERANCE`, spec §5.1) — the intent exists; it is just not
applied across sources at read time. Fixing it means changing verdict semantics
app-wide, which deserves its own review rather than riding along in a data
commit. **Recommended follow-up: collapse consecutive within-tolerance
observations before selecting the comparison pair, in `ShrinkDetector.analyze`,
`previousAcceptedQuantity` and `hit_rate.verdict` together.** That single change
would take this number to 25/25 honestly.

## 6. A live bug found on the way: `/v1/feed` was publishing a −94.1 % that never happened

Browse was showing:

```
DOUBLE STUF CHOCOLATE SANDWICH COO | 0044000060251 | kroger | 530 -> 31.468 mass | -94.1%
```

Nothing shrinks by 94 %. Kroger's own product record gives that pack's size as
**"1.11 oz"**, so a Kroger observation of 31.468 g landed against the 530 g FDC
row and the feed published the difference as a verified case.

`ShrinkDetector.analyze` already refuses a cross-source pair whose ratio falls
outside 0.25–4 (its C2 clamp) and renders `.insufficientData`. `buildFeed` had no
such guard — and it is the more consequential of the two, because it is what
publishes a claim to every Browse tab rather than to one screen. Commit `3eab74f`
ports the same 0.25 floor to the database path, with two tests. Curated rows stay
exempt: they are same-source, hand-checked pairs with a citation, so a genuinely
documented collapse is still publishable.
